01 — Air Conditioning

Cooling that keeps up with a Carolina August

When a system quits, the first question is what actually failed — not how fast we can sell you a new one. Most no-cool calls come down to a capacitor, a contactor, a clogged drain, or a low charge from a leak that needs finding rather than topping off.

When replacement genuinely is the right call, we do a Manual J load calculation first. Lake-area homes are frequently running equipment one to two tons larger than the house needs, which is exactly why they feel cold and damp at the same time.

  • Same-day diagnostics with flat-rate pricing
  • Refrigerant leak detection, not just a recharge
  • Full system replacement with load calculation

02 — Heating & Furnaces

Warm through every cold snap we get

We don't get Minnesota winters, but we do get a handful of nights in the teens — and those are exactly the nights a marginal furnace decides to quit. Annual heating inspections catch cracked heat exchangers, failing igniters, and blocked flues before they become a carbon monoxide problem or a midnight emergency.

We service and install gas furnaces, electric air handlers, and dual-fuel setups that switch between a heat pump and gas backup at whatever outdoor temperature actually makes economic sense for your utility rates.

  • Combustion analysis and safety inspection
  • Heat exchanger and CO testing
  • Dual-fuel changeover optimisation
Call for heating service

03 — Heat Pumps

The right technology for this exact climate

North Carolina is close to ideal heat pump territory. Modern inverter-driven units hold full capacity down into the teens, run quietly, and modulate instead of slamming on and off — which is what makes them so much better at wringing humidity out of the air than a single-stage unit ever was.

For most homes around the lake, a variable-speed heat pump is both the cheapest system to run and the most comfortable to live with. We'll show you the operating-cost math against your current setup before you commit.

  • Variable-speed and cold-climate models
  • Defrost and auxiliary heat set up correctly
  • Rebate and tax-credit paperwork handled
