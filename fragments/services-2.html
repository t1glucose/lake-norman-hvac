05 — Indoor Air Quality

The lake gives you humidity. We take it back out.

If your house sits at 60% relative humidity in July, it will feel clammy at 72°F and you'll keep pushing the thermostat down chasing a feeling that isn't really about temperature. That's an expensive way to stay uncomfortable.

A whole-home dehumidifier tied into the return handles it directly, and usually lets you run the thermostat two or three degrees warmer while feeling better. We also fit deep-media filtration and UV treatment for coil hygiene, which matters more here than most places — damp coils grow things.

  • Whole-home dehumidification
  • 4- and 5-inch media filtration
  • UV coil treatment and crawlspace assessment

06 — Ductwork

The best equipment can't fix bad duct

A typical older duct system leaks 20–30% of the air it carries into the crawlspace or attic. You paid to condition that air. Worse, what leaks out gets replaced by air pulled in from those same spaces — which is how crawlspace smell ends up in a bedroom.

We test, seal, redesign undersized returns, and replace runs that are past saving. On a lot of houses this is a bigger comfort win than new equipment, for a fraction of the cost.

  • Duct leakage testing and sealing
  • Return-air sizing corrections
  • Full replacement and redesign

Common questions

Straight answers

How much does a new system cost?

It depends on the size of the house, the equipment tier, and what the existing ductwork needs — so anyone quoting a number over the phone is guessing. We give a written, itemised, fixed price after seeing the house, and that price is what you pay.

Do you charge extra for nights and weekends?

No. There is no overtime rate and no emergency surcharge, for anyone, member or not. A 10pm Saturday call is billed at the same rate as Tuesday morning.

How often should I service my system?

Twice a year — cooling in spring, heating in autumn. It is not an upsell ritual; it is how you find the failing part in April instead of at the worst possible moment in July.

My house feels cold and clammy at the same time. Why?

Almost always an oversized system. It satisfies the thermostat fast, shuts off, and never runs long enough to pull moisture out of the air. It is one of the most common problems we find in lake-area homes, and it is fixable.
